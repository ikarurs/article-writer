# Analysis check: municipality-level revision

## Publication-readiness verdict

**The package now meets the requested municipality resolution for descriptive political outcomes, but it is not yet publication-ready and still does not test an energy-price channel.** It contains municipality panels for an early-invasion election (Schleswig-Holstein Landtag, May 2022), a post-break election (Schleswig-Holstein local, May 2023), and a later election (Brandenburg Landtag, September 2024). Lower Saxony and Berlin should remain context only. There is still no municipality/Kreis election panel in the June--December 2022 anticipation/high-price phase; the October 2022 Lower Saxony result is only at constituency level.

The data support **descriptive statistics and exploratory maps only**. They do not support a price regression, mediation analysis, difference-in-differences estimate, or causal claim because no local retail-price panel or demonstrated price first stage is present. Refinery distance is an exploratory geographic descriptor, not treatment.

Publication should wait until the blocking checks below are resolved. Once resolved, the current material is publishable as a transparent descriptive/feasibility article, not as the proposed causal article.

## Numerical re-check

Independent reads of the processed CSVs confirm the headline numbers in `outputs/findings.md`:

- Schleswig-Holstein Landtag: 1,097 unique municipality keys; median AfD change -0.500085 pp, CDU +10.755961 pp, SPD -10.811523 pp. The matched subset contains 2,178,975 eligible voters in 2017, equal to the reported 94.00% of the raw 2017 total (2,318,022).
- Schleswig-Holstein local: 1,074 unique municipality keys; median turnout change +1.981554 pp. Non-missing party-change samples reproduce the reported candidature coverage: SPD 343, CDU 457, Greens 105, FDP 91, AfD 5, Left 17.
- Brandenburg Landtag: 409 unique municipality keys; median AfD change +8.810573 pp. The four distance-quartile means reproduce as 10.42545, 7.86073, 8.73260, and 9.73614 pp, so the stated non-monotonic pattern is numerically accurate.
- No duplicate or missing municipality keys were found. The Brandenburg numerical fields have no missing values, and processed turnout shares lie within 0--100.

These checks confirm calculations in the generated files; they do not independently validate that every raw workbook column and geographic match was interpreted correctly.

## Blocking checks before publication

1. **Rerun the analysis and package check.** `analysis/analysis.py` is newer than the generated outputs. For example, the code now validates Lower Saxony against independent official reports, while `outputs/tables/election_validation.csv` still contains the earlier validation label. The committed outputs must be generated by the current code.
2. **Add independent aggregate validation for all three municipality panels.** `election_validation.csv` currently covers only Lower Saxony and Berlin, not Schleswig-Holstein or Brandenburg. For each election year, compare raw parsed totals for eligible voters, voters, valid votes, and selected parties with an independent official statewide final-result table. This is especially important because the Schleswig-Holstein 2018 workbook and Brandenburg workbooks use hard-coded column positions.
3. **Validate the Schleswig-Holstein Landtag crosswalk.** The code treats identical `Wahlbezirk Nr.` values as stable geography, but a repeated identifier does not prove unchanged precinct boundaries or residential composition. Verify this using official boundary/crosswalk documentation. Until then, rename “stable precincts” to “common precinct identifiers” and describe the result as matched-subset municipality aggregates. Report both 2017 and 2022 electorate coverage and the number/electorate share of excluded districts.
4. **Document municipality changes and vote coverage.** For Schleswig-Holstein local elections, report the unmatched 2018 and 2023 municipalities, the matched electorate share in both years, and how mergers/key changes were handled. For Brandenburg, report the names/keys and electorate shares of the four excluded changed municipalities, not only their count.
5. **Validate row types and postal votes.** Confirm from each field dictionary that the result rows aggregated are mutually exclusive and that postal/special districts are neither duplicated nor incorrectly assigned to residential municipalities. State the allocation/exclusion rule in the processed-data notes.
6. **Add count-integrity assertions.** Require positive eligible/valid denominators, voters no greater than eligible voters, nonnegative party counts, and selected-party counts no greater than valid votes. Current spatial routines mostly validate only derived shares.
7. **Verify geometry vintage and distance definition.** Record the actual BKG feature vintage rather than hard-coding `geography_year: 2025`. Label `distance_pck_km` as straight-line distance from a polygon representative point to the PRTR facility coordinate. Add a sensitivity check using centroid or nearest-boundary distance before drawing substantive conclusions from quartiles.
8. **Treat small municipalities explicitly.** The unweighted municipality summaries give a tiny municipality the same weight as a city and produce volatile extremes. Label quartile means and medians as unweighted; report an electorate-weighted sensitivity and/or a pre-specified minimum valid-vote threshold. Do not suppress the unweighted result, but show that conclusions are not driven by small denominators.
9. **Fix encoding and presentation.** `outputs/findings.md` ends with garbled quotation marks, and source/metadata files still contain mojibake such as `Tankerkönig`/`Hövermann` rendered incorrectly. Correct these before publication. The two spatial PNGs render clearly and accurately label the maps as descriptive.

## Claims that are supportable now

Subject to the validation above, the article may report:

- descriptive municipality-level vote-share or turnout changes between elections of the same type;
- the exact matched-unit counts and disclosed coverage;
- that the published WSI AfD point estimate was higher in November 2022 than in January 2022;
- that unweighted Brandenburg distance quartiles are non-monotonic in the current exploratory construction; and
- that the observed timing and maps do not isolate a war, anticipation, sanctions, or price mechanism.

Use “the median municipality in the matched sample” rather than implying a statewide voter effect. For Schleswig-Holstein May 2022, prefer “the five-year matched-sample median AfD change was -0.50 pp” over “there was no early local AfD surge”; the design cannot isolate an invasion effect. For Brandenburg, “no monotonic pattern in unweighted quartile summaries” is safer than claiming that distance has no relationship with voting.

## Claims that remain blocked

Do not claim that municipalities near PCK, a pipeline, Russia, or any refinery experienced a larger energy-price shock; that the January 2023 purchase stop caused local prices to diverge; that local energy costs caused AfD or other party support to change; or that energy prices mediate the political response to the invasion. Do not compare the municipality changes mechanically with Konc et al.'s 7.5-point household treatment estimate.

Those claims require a station-level or otherwise credible local retail-price panel on the same stable geography, reconstruction of prevailing prices, pre-trend checks, and a demonstrated divergence around the relevant events. Only after that first stage should the article estimate election-pair fixed-effect or exposure-gradient specifications. Pipeline/refinery proximity alone is not a substitute.

## Remaining scope gap

The revised package covers municipality outcomes in May 2022, May 2023, and September 2024, but not municipality/Kreis outcomes in the key 3 June--31 December 2022 anticipation phase. If the article promises phase-by-phase spatial evidence, add a reproducible municipality/Kreis result for that interval or state explicitly that the anticipation phase is observed only through national WSI points and Lower Saxony constituencies.

## SPUR addendum

The rerun now includes `spur-python` 0.1.1 and `scpc-python` 0.1.2 on 409 Brandenburg municipalities. The code follows the documented 10% rule: I(0) p=0.3555 and I(1) p=0.1157, so the jointly transformed branch is selected. Its proximity coefficient is -3.6081 with SCPC 95% interval [-10.5293, 3.3132]; the reported but unselected levels branch is 0.5562 with interval [-1.8801, 2.9925]. The generated diagnostics, regression CSV, and summary agree with the rewritten findings. This is an appropriate spatial-persistence robustness check, not a remedy for the absent price first stage or spatial confounding. The descriptive package can report it with those limits; an energy-price-effect article remains blocked.

The current run also adds count-integrity assertions, valid-vote-weighted distance-quartile means, and a representative-point versus centroid sensitivity. These address part of the earlier audit. Independent SH/Brandenburg aggregate validation, formal precinct-boundary documentation, and full unmatched-vote accounting remain publication-quality upgrades; the package appropriately labels the present result as descriptive rather than causal.
